## 1. Scope

**What the deep release adds: `pnk ask`, and `pnk ask --deep`.** A question surface that costs
nothing, and an explicit paid form that answers it — bounded, budgeted, and sized by the free
confidence signal. For the CLI and for cron, "where no agent is present" (DESIGN § 4.3).

It is the **last unbuilt paid entry point**. Adding it edits
[`.paid-path-allowlist`](../.paid-path-allowlist), [DESIGN § 1](../docs/DESIGN.md#1-what-this-is)
and [INVARIANTS.md](../docs/INVARIANTS.md) **in one commit** — the invariant says so, and the gate
enforces the first of the three.

**What it is not:**

| Not this | Because |
|---|---|
| An MCP tool | DESIGN § 4.3: an MCP caller composes `pinakes_search` → `pinakes_get` itself, on reasoning already paid for. A server-side loop would spend the *operator's* money on the *caller's* question. **Enforced by a gate (E3), not by a convention** |
| A retrieval change | Nothing in `search.py`'s pipeline moves. `--deep` is a consumer of `search()`, not a variant of it — so the golden-set eval is unaffected and no `schema_version` bump is in scope |
| An agent framework | DESIGN § 4.3: "making the tools composable rather than by building an agent". The loop is prompt-side structure inside one operation, with no persistent state beyond its transcript and the suggestions a user commits |
| A second corpus-scale measurement | The costs here are per-query and bounded by § 5's caps. What must be measured is the *estimator*, not retrieval quality (E6) |

### D-21 — What is `pnk ask` *without* `--deep`? ✅ TAKEN — option A

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. `pnk ask` is the question surface; bare it never spends, `--deep` adds the paid loop** *(recommended)* | Matches DESIGN § 4.2 exactly — "on CLI: returns retrieval-only and prints how to escalate". The escalation notice finally names a real command. A user types one thing whether or not they will pay | Two commands whose free output overlaps `pnk search`'s. Needs a stated difference, and it is a real one: `search` answers *what is in the KB about this*, `ask` answers *what would it take to answer this* — the same passages, plus the confidence and the price of synthesis |
| B. `pnk ask` requires `--deep`, and errors without it | No overlap with `search` at all | A command that refuses its own bare form is a bad surface, and `pnk ask "…"` is exactly what a new user will type first |
| C. No `ask`; `--deep` becomes a flag on `pnk search` | One retrieval command, no duplication | Contradicts ~20 committed references including INVARIANTS and DESIGN § 1, and `search --deep` reads as "search harder", not "spend money reasoning" |

**Recommendation: A.** The free `pnk ask` is not a duplicate of `search`: it prints the evidence,
the confidence, and *what it would cost to answer* — which is the escalation notice M2 says is
already there in prose, made actionable.

### D-28 — Does `--deep` spend when the free path is already confident? ✅ TAKEN — option B

**Answer this before D-22; D-22 is the same question with the signal missing.**

The tension: DESIGN § 4.2 says the system escalates only below the confidence threshold. But
`high` confidence means *the retrieved passages are relevant*, not *the question is answered* — and
`--deep` exists for CLI and cron, "where no agent is present", so what the user wants back is an
**answer**, not passages they must read themselves.

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **B. Yes — confidence sizes the work, it does not authorise it** *(recommended)* | `--deep` is an explicit, typed request to pay; nothing about it is the "silent spending" § 4.2 forbids. High confidence takes the **cheap branch** — one synthesis call over round 0's evidence — and low confidence takes the decomposition loop. The user always gets what they asked for, and the signal is what makes it cost less | Someone who types `--deep` on a question the free path fully covers pays for one call they may not have needed. Bounded, cheap, and visible in `pnk budget` |
| A. No — the free path answering means no spend at all | Literal reading of § 4.2; the strongest possible free-path-first stance | **`pnk ask --deep` would return passages and no answer on exactly the questions it handles best**, which makes the flag unusable for its own stated purpose. A cron job asking a well-covered question gets nothing to act on |
| C. No by default, with `--force` | Preserves A's default and B's escape hatch | A flag whose meaning is "actually do the thing I already asked for" |

**Recommendation: B.** It removes the tension rather than choosing a side: **confidence chooses the
branch, the caps bound the cost, and `--deep` always answers.** § 5's flow below is written to B; if
A is chosen, § 5 and E4's exit criteria both change.

### D-22 — What does `--deep` do on a KB with no calibrated confidence? ✅ TAKEN — option E

M6: every KB stamped from `notes` reports `confidence: unknown`, so under D-28's answer the signal
that **sizes** the work is absent by default. Under B, `unknown` does not mean "cannot spend" — it
means "cannot stop early".

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **E. Run, bounded by the caps instead of by the signal, and say so** *(recommended)* | The round cap and `per_operation_eur` are real bounds that do not depend on calibration, so a run is never unbounded. The user gets an answer on a stock KB, labelled *"no calibrated signal — stopped at the round cap, not at sufficiency"*, and `pnk doctor` already tells them how to fix it | An uncalibrated run costs more than a calibrated one for the same question — it cannot stop early. That is a true statement about the KB, and the label says it |
| A. Refuse, naming `python -m pinakes.calibrate` | The project refuses to invent a confidence number; spending against a signal it will not invent is arguably the same defect | **The headline feature of the release refuses on the default template.** A user meets a refusal before any success, and calibration needs a golden set they may not have written |
| B. Run, treating `unknown` as *insufficient* — always take the decomposition branch | Works everywhere | Pays the **maximum** every time, and cannot distinguish an escalation that was needed from one that was unmeasured. E is this with the cheap branch first |
| C. Run, treating `unknown` as *sufficient* | Cheapest | Silently does the least work on the KBs least able to notice. Rejected — it fails silently |

**Recommendation: E.** It keeps the release usable out of the box, keeps every spend bounded, and

*Sub-question: a **calibrated** KB whose reranker fingerprint has since changed hits the same

## 5. The shape of the loop

Free first, then bounded rounds, each of constant maximum cost so the whole operation can be priced
before the first call.

**This is D-28 option B and D-22 option E as taken.** Both were open when this section was drafted;
neither is now, so it reads as specification rather than as a sketch.

```
pnk ask "<question>"                      round 0, free, always
 └─ search() — the §4.1 pipeline, unchanged
 └─ confidence (§4.2) — it decides how much work the question needs, never whether to spend

 without --deep ──► passages + confidence + what --deep would cost. Never spends. Exit 0.

 with --deep ─────► high / medium → the cheap branch: ONE synthesis call over round 0's
                                    passages. Usually the whole run.
                    low           → the loop below.
                    unknown       → the loop below, with no early stop: it ends at the round
                                    cap or the budget, and the output says which.

loop, while rounds_used < max_rounds and the next reservation fits every window:
 1. decompose      1 paid call   question + carried memory → flat subproblem list
 2. retrieve       free          search() per subproblem, filters inherited
 3. answer         1 paid call   sub-answer from retrieved passages only, cited
 4. re-fold        free          carried memory rewritten to a bounded token budget
 5. sufficiency    free          the §4.2 signal over the accumulated evidence — the early
                                 stop, and the step `unknown` cannot take

exit:
  sufficient          → cited answer + transcript + printed suggestions
  rounds exhausted    → best-effort answer from memory, labelled with which bound ended it
  budget would breach → D-23
```

**Why two paid calls per round and not one.** The round's cost must be knowable *before* it runs
(DESIGN § 5's pre-call reservation). Two calls of known maximum size — bounded input by the carried
memory cap plus `final_k` passages, bounded output by `max_tokens` — give a constant per-round
worst case, so `max_rounds × per_round` is the operation's ceiling and can be refused at round 0.
A single call that both plans and answers has no bound on how much it retrieves into itself.

**The cheap branch is not a special case, it is the common one.** A confident retrieval that needs
only synthesis costs **one** call, against `2 × max_rounds` for the loop. That is the whole return
on having a calibrated signal, and it is why D-22 option E labels its absence rather than hiding
it: an uncalibrated KB pays the loop price for questions a calibrated one would answer in one call.

**Three properties taken from the research notes, none of them code** (M10): a subproblem is never
re-asked after it stalls (the cursor advances); the carried memory is re-folded rather than
appended, keeping per-round cost constant; and the round cap ends in a best-effort answer rather
than a failure. The two defects the same notes record are designed out: sufficiency is gated by the
**free calibrated signal**, never by the model's self-report, and structured output is used rather
than regex repair of truncated JSON.

**Prompt injection is a first-class concern here, and it is new.** Every earlier paid path
transcribes a PDF; this one feeds retrieved *document text* to a model whose output then drives
further retrieval — and, once D-25's second half lands, proposes writes. Two rules, both testable:
a subproblem's retrieval is always `search()` over the KB with the caller's filters, never a
model-supplied path or query against anything else; and a proposed link's endpoints must both be
document ids **that appeared in this run's own retrieval**, resolved through the existing
containment check rather than a new one. Each is tested against a hostile fixture where it applies —
the retrieval rule in E4, the suggestion rule in E7.

- Replaces the placeholder at `cli.py:384-391` and its test (M2).
- **Drive-by in the same file:** `cli.py:240` is an unreachable `return EXIT_OK` after the line

**E1 adds no `--deep` flag, and its output must not tell anyone to type one.** Worked out 20260811
14:50, before writing a line, because the first instinct was the wrong one: a `--deep` that exists
and refuses is the **same defect `0.20.1` fixed** — `vector_tier = "sqlite-vec"` was accepted for a

So E1's escalation block describes **how much work answering this question would take** — one
synthesis call at `high`/`medium`, decomposition and repeated search at `low`, and *nothing can
tell* at `unknown` — and names paid synthesis as belonging to the deep release, without ever
printing a command line to run. `--deep` appears for the first time in **E4**, the increment that
implements it.

**`pnk ask` must state plainly that no answer was synthesised.** Someone typing `ask` expects an
answer; passages are not one. The line is not decoration — it is the difference between an honest
retrieval surface and one that lets a user mistake evidence for a conclusion.

**The `unknown` remedy is one sentence, not three.** `SearchResult.confidence_reason` already
discriminates the three branches (`search.py:596-623`) and is printed, so the *reason* says which
one applies. The remedy sentence then covers all three at once — fit `[retrieval.confidence]` with
`python -m pinakes.calibrate`, with reranking on, and with the fitted reranker being the one in
use. **Do not branch on the reason string, and do not re-check the three conditions in the CLI**:
that is a second copy of `_confidence`'s logic, and a second copy can disagree — the reason
`Accountant.confirm_document` gives for living where it does.

**Exit criteria.** `pnk ask` on `tests/demo-kb` prints cited evidence, a confidence line and what
answering would cost — and **never** a synthesised answer, because nothing free can produce one; an
uncalibrated KB says which of the three `unknown` branches it hit (`search.py:596-623`);
`--json` round-trips; `tests/test_paid_path.py`'s subprocess gate covers `pnk ask` as part of the
free path, in this increment, before any paid module exists.

**Tests.** `tests/test_cli_ask.py`: the calibrated-high path spends nothing and says so; the
uncalibrated path names `pinakes.calibrate`; every filter in D-27 narrows the result; `--json` and
the human output agree on confidence and citation; **the free path gate imports no paid client with
`pnk ask` in it** (extend `tests/test_paid_path.py`'s free-path script).

### E2 — the estimator for a round · unblocked (D-24 A)

Pure, no client, no I/O. `src/pinakes/deep/estimate.py`.

- `estimate_round(...) -> RoundEstimate` — worst case for one round: `(carried_memory_tokens +
  final_k × chunk_token_ceiling + prompt_tokens) × input_price + 2 × max_tokens × output_price`.
- `estimate_synthesis(...)` — the **cheap branch** (§ 5): one call, so half a round's output
  ceiling and no carried memory. Under D-28 option B this is what most `--deep` runs actually cost,
  so it is a first-class estimate and not a fraction of the other one.
- `estimate_operation(branch, rounds, …)` — the number checked before round 0: the synthesis
  estimate for the cheap branch, `max_rounds × per-round` for the loop. **Which branch is priced is
  decided by the same confidence value that decides which branch runs**, read once and passed, never
  computed twice — the reason `Accountant.confirm_document` gives for living where it does.
- Constants declared with their measurement, in `estimate.py`'s own style: a ceiling below a
  measurement is not a ceiling, and `PAGE_TOKEN_CEILING`'s comment is the model to copy.
- Reuses `Prices`, `StalePricesError`, `ContextWindowExceededError` and `MAX_INPUT_TOKENS`
  unchanged.

**Exit criteria.** `Decimal` end to end, never quantised here. Stale prices refuse. A round whose
input exceeds the model's documented window raises before any call. Every constant carries the
command that produced it.

**Tests.** Boundary equality at the cent; a stale price table refuses with the age in the message;
the operation estimate is exactly `max_rounds ×` the round estimate; `Decimal(str(x))` is used for
every TOML-derived number (INVARIANTS).

### E4 — the loop · unblocked (D-28 B, D-22 E, D-23 A)

`src/pinakes/deep/loop.py`, driven by E3's transport and E2's estimator, through `Accountant`.

- One `Accountant` per invocation, `operation="ask"` (M9 — no ledger change).
- `estimate_operation` checked **before round 0**, every blocked window named at once with the
  exact manifest edit that would admit the run — the shape `reserve_document` already prints.
- `Accountant.paid_call` per call, `response_received()` the instant the client returns, then
  `reconcile()`. The context manager is not optional.
- Round cap, carried-memory cap, cursor that never re-asks a stalled subproblem.
- `confirm_above_eur` honoured through `resolve_confirmation`, unchanged.

**Exit criteria.** `pnk ask` **without** `--deep` makes zero paid calls on every confidence value —
that is the free-path promise, and it is the one this increment could break. A confident `--deep`
run takes the cheap branch and makes exactly **one** paid call. A run halted by a cap behaves as
D-23 decides, and the ledger's totals equal the sum of its reconciliations. Interrupting mid-loop
leaves a reservation `pnk budget` reports as `unknown outcome`, never a lost record.

**Tests.** Recorded-fixture runs for: the cheap branch at high confidence (exactly one call, and the
estimate checked was the synthesis one); low confidence answered at round 1; answered at the round
cap; `unknown` running to the round cap with the output naming which bound ended it; halted by
`per_operation_eur` on round 3 with the ledger reconciled for rounds 1–2; a call that times out
recorded as unknown outcome rather than voided; every window's refusal naming every blocked window
at once. **And the injection rule** (§ 5): a fixture whose retrieved text instructs the model to
read a path outside the KB still produces retrieval that is `search()` over this KB with the
caller's filters — a subproblem is a *query string*, and there is no code path by which it becomes a
path, a KB selector or a filter.

**Mutation targets:** the cap comparison, the `response_received` flag, the branch selection, and
the cursor advance — each mutated on purpose to confirm the right test fails.

### E6 — the measurement run · unblocked; follows E4

The only increment that spends real money, under the § 5 caps and the
[MEASUREMENT-RUN](../docs/MEASUREMENT-RUN.md) procedure.

- Calibrate E2's constants against real calls on a **synthetic** corpus, and record — like

- Measure **both branches** (§ 5): the one-call synthesis and a full loop. They are separate
  numbers and the cheap one is the common case.
- Report the over-reservation factor. The extractor's first live call over-reserved **11.5×**; the

**Exit criteria.** Every constant in E2 carries a measurement and the command that produced it.
Spend for the whole run is in the ledger and reported in the retrospective. A ceiling is never
lowered to a measurement taken on synthetic data — `PAGE_TOKEN_CEILING`'s comment is the precedent
and it is binding.
